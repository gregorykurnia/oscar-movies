# Oscar Movies: Design Brief

**Status:** Answered 2026-10-22 (Step 4). Input for the three design directions (Step 5). The binding visual rules come later, in the design guide (Step 6).

## The owner's brief

Owner's words: "I don't like cartoony/gimmicky things, I want this to look modern. Like modern fonts, modern look, beautiful UI. Don't want it to be old fashioned, playful, traditional etc. Also I want it to be light look."

- **Personality:** calm and warm, modern. A beautiful UI with a **light** look.
- **Not:** cartoony, gimmicky, old-fashioned, traditional or playful.
- **Density:** balanced. Roomy rows on the race; the year grid and the watch list can be denser.
- **Colour:** restrained. White and soft greys, with one accent in Oscar gold for wins, the current stage and actions. The film flags supply the rest of the colour.
- **Type:** clean, modern sans-serif throughout.
- **Light or dark:** light only. Dark mode is not designed. Adding it later is a Step 12 design addition, if the owner asks.
- **References:** none named. The owner is happy for the directions to explore.
- **Voice** (not asked; inferred from "not playful"): plain and brief, warm, no jokes. Confirm at review.

## Name, logo and must-haves

- **Name:** Oscar Movies (working name).
- **Logo:** a small logo mark beside the name, used for the favicon and the app icon. The mark is designed in Step 6.
- **Must appear:** TMDB's notice ("uses TMDB but is not endorsed") and TMDB's logo on the Sources section, as TMDB's terms require. The Wikipedia credit (CC BY-SA 4.0) goes on the same section (product plan §3.12).
- **Must not appear:** film posters or other film artwork. The product plan declined them (2026-10-22), so the look comes from type, colour, flags and scores.
- **Other third-party logos:** none (default; none were asked for).

## The #1 screen: the Oscar race (home)

The most frequent job in the product plan ("Check this year's Oscar race", daily in season, §2) is the home screen (§3.3). The three directions each show it, with the Best Picture tab open.

- Opens on the current season (2026). A year switcher reaches any year from 2012.
- Four categories, each its own section (tabs on phone): Best Picture, Documentary Feature, International Feature, Animated Feature.
- At a glance: the contenders, their Oscar stage and the **% chance to win** (with a bar). The precursor results and scores come second.
- Each contender: flag, title, Oscar stage, % chance to win, % chance to be nominated (until the nominations are announced), main precursor results ("Won: BAFTA, PGA · Nominated: Globes"), average score with its source count ("84 · 3 of 4"), and one-tap watched and want-to-watch marks.
- Each category shows its stage line, for example "Shortlist out 16 Dec · Nominations 21 Jan".
- A % opens a short "why": the past seasons and results it is based on.
- States to design: nothing yet ("No contenders yet: the first precursor results arrive in <month>."); "Early: few results yet"; after the nominations, only the nominees; after the ceremony, the winner is marked and the % shows as "final prediction".
- Search is on every page, and the race is one tap from anywhere (§3.14, §2).

## For the three directions (Step 5)

- Three directions, all within this brief: calm, warm, modern, light, balanced, restrained colour, clean sans-serif, nothing playful or traditional. They differ in how people move between the four categories and how a contender's numbers are laid out, not in mood.
- Each uses realistic mock data from the product plan, never lorem ipsum.
